# Post-Ship Work

These items remain deferred until the exploratory Replay 04 V1 artifact ships. Nothing in this file authorizes a run or makes later execution mandatory.

## Future Program Map

This map organizes possible future research; V1 did not validate every layer, and one experiment may span several layers. The owner will later choose which experiments are worth running.

```text
MESSY INFORMATION ENVIRONMENT
        |
        v
EVIDENCE ACQUISITION - What did the agent find?
        |
        v
WORLD-STATE CONSTRUCTION - What did the agent believe was true?
        |
        v
EVIDENCE INTEGRATION - What evidence actually affected its reasoning?
        |
        v
HUMAN-STATE ELICITATION - What did it know about the person, and what did it need to ask?
        |
        v
DECISION POLICY - What did it recommend, under what assumptions, and why?
        |
        v
EPISTEMIC POSTURE - What did it present as fact, inference, uncertainty, preference, or judgment, and how much authority did its presentation imply?
        |
        v
ACTION / AUTHORITY - Did it preserve human authority and prepare or execute appropriately?
        |
        v
ROBUSTNESS - Would the behavior survive another run, model, provider, decision, information environment, or user?
```

The broad question is: "How reliably can an AI agent move from a messy information environment to a consequential human decision, and what evidence should we require before trusting the work between those endpoints?" Existing robustness items collectively test survival across repetition, model or provider, decision, operator, information environment, and search or reasoning procedure.

Deal Autopsy V1 is the first published exploratory study from this possible broader effort, not its culmination. Its five-run pilot surfaced retrieval consistency, evidence integration, evidence dependence and context value, owner-state elicitation, epistemic posture, and treatment-effort confounds as questions worth separating. Its findings are independently useful; those questions call for redesigned follow-up experiments rather than mechanical completion of the stale larger grid.

1. **Replay 01 expansion and posture/claim discipline:** Revisit the operational outcome-execution replay after V1 under the previously approved A/B/C, three-tier, three-repeat architecture or a separately approved redesign. Test whether an agent distinguishes what the operator said, including plans, intentions, negotiation posture, or strategic elicitation, from established world facts and the operator's underlying outcome. The “I am currently renovating” case should test whether sell-as-is or minimal-repair options are wrongly eliminated. Sanitized posture examples may be public; any sensitive personal motivation remains private ground truth.
2. **Clarification-only ablation:** Add unstructured redacted context plus targeted questions without the five-part brief to isolate clarification, then compare adaptive dialogue with the current pilot's batched lower bound.
3. **Persistent operator-profile experiment:** Test whether provenance-linked stable preferences reduce clarification burden without reducing decision quality.
4. **Population and personal priors:** Compare generic workflow priors with user-history priors while guarding against stale or paternalistic assumptions.
5. **Mispersonalization safeguards:** Test provenance, confidence, scope, freshness, contradiction handling, and owner override as controls on inferred preferences.

Together, items 2–5 study the human-state elicitation layer: what historical records can recover, what belongs in a persistent profile, what can safely be inferred from personal history, what must be asked contemporaneously, and how stale or incorrect personalization is challenged and overridden.

6. **Cross-provider robustness check:** Preserve the Tier 2 plan to repeat Replay 04 across A/B/C for three repeats with another provider's frontier model; the current pilot does not establish model- or provider-general effects.
7. **Additional replays and classification:** Add Replays 02, 03, and 05, then defer broader primitive classification and the capability matrix until their evidence exists.
8. **Dubai trajectory:** Add the Dubai corpus as a second real-world decision-and-outcome trajectory after V1.
9. **Design-partner replication:** Apply the method to one bounded workflow from another operator to test whether the observed interface effects transfer.
10. **GUI and desktop execution:** Treat live computer action as a complementary future layer after the static decision and work-product experiment is credible. If the project evolves into an interface, investigate exposing the work between evidence and action rather than merely displaying polished final prose: AI workpapers, decision provenance and evidence lineage, agent observability, claim-to-source links, visible uncertainty and unknowns, reconstructed world state, evidence that entered downstream reasoning, changes caused by clarification or human-state elicitation, fact-versus-inference-versus-judgment distinctions, epistemic posture, recommended action, and action-authority boundaries. The motivating human-facing question is: "What should a person be able to inspect before trusting a consequential agent recommendation?" V1 does not establish that this is the correct interface solution.
11. **Test-time search, persistence, reflection, and verification:** Separate six mechanisms that “think longer” claims often combine: a larger per-call search/reasoning budget; persistent continuation that retains the prior trajectory and failures without new owner information; explicit reflection on why the prior strategy failed; fresh independent restarts at approximately matched total compute; continuation after a verifier or human identifies a concrete defect; and demand pressure to continue toward completeness. Precommit rounds, attempts, or total budget and preserve every trajectory rather than continuing until success. Track visible fact recovery, downstream use, source coverage, unsupported claims, claim-versus-fact discipline, retrieval completion, tool-state failures, and decision quality. Citation breadth shows visible coverage, not completeness, correctness, weighting, integration, or reliability. Test demand pressure for both persistence benefits and fabrication, unsupported certainty, premature conclusions, or honesty failures. Transfer from mathematical-search anecdotes remains a hypothesis.
12. **Matched-effort natural request (A′):** Compare a natural request with approximately the same extra search/reasoning opportunity as B, but without requiring B's structured intermediate representation. Current Replay 04 A-versus-B evidence cannot separate benefit from structure itself from benefit from another opportunity to work.
13. **Run-to-run retrieval consistency:** Use enough precommitted independent repetitions to estimate visible recovery variability under an identical treatment, how often evidence is omitted or surfaced but ignored, and how much repetition reduces uncertainty about one run. The current structured outcomes of 1/2, 1/2 with a different communication, and 2/2 are three run-level observations, not a retrieval-rate estimate; do not choose a sample size here.
14. **Larger Replay 04 designs:** Preserve the unexecuted A3/B3/C2/C3 pilot remainder and the 27-attempt Tier 1a grid as deferred historical designs, not unfinished V1 obligations. Before reviving either, explicitly reaffirm or redesign it using the pilot findings rather than mechanically completing a stale plan.
15. **Project-name decision:** Before the first durable public release or outreach, use one small fixed time budget to consider two naming levels: the broader research-program name and the title of the first published study or Replay 04 V1 artifact. “Deal Autopsy” may be too deal-specific for an eventual umbrella program while remaining useful for Study 01; one possible structure is `[broader program name] — Study 01: Deal Autopsy — [study-specific title]`. “The Plausibility Trap” is a strong candidate framing or title for Study 01 because polished, reasonable-looking answers can conceal materially different or incomplete underlying work, but it is not the settled umbrella thesis. If no clearly better name appears within the cap, keep the existing name and ship. Any later rename should be a repository-wide consistency migration, not a partial rename.
16. **Public user-directed-agent framing:** Explain in plain language whether an AI can recover what the user is trying to accomplish rather than merely optimize the literal requested means, while preserving human authority over consequential decisions. Use objective recovery, intent discovery, evidence organization, ranked unknowns, clarification, decision quality, concrete work products, artifact quality, authority calibration, and verification conditions. The scaffold remains a floor: a model may challenge a false premise, identify a proxy goal, or propose a better non-obvious means. Do not call this a general alignment benchmark or change the experiment to fit the framing.
17. **Persistent-agent implications and counter-risk:** Anchor a company-independent implications section to the observed fact that C1 clarification elicited relevant contemporaneous owner state absent from the supplied context. Treat reduced repeated intent-transfer burden as a hypothesis, alongside the risk that a stale, incorrect, or overconfident user model compounds errors. The present case study does not test persistent memory, personalization, local inference, data sovereignty, or user-owned infrastructure; items 3–5 define the experiments and safeguards needed before making those claims.
18. **Minimal-context and evidence-dependence ablation:** Replay 04 V1 had no minimal-context baseline, and all five evidence-rich runs gave relatively similar opening recommendations. A generic model given only a few obvious facts about a fresh property offer might independently recommend negotiating or countering, perhaps in a similar range, so V1 cannot determine how much the historical evidence caused or changed the recommendation. A future approved design could compare bare decision facts, a concise factual summary, fuller historical evidence, targeted removal of decision-relevant evidence, and carefully designed counterfactual evidence perturbations without precommitting those conditions or a sample size here. Measure factual reasoning, fallback policy, confidence and calibration, visible evidence support, assumptions, uncertainty, and downstream changes caused by decision-relevant evidence. An answer can be compatible with the evidence without demonstrating that it depended on or meaningfully used the evidence. Core question: "How do we distinguish a recommendation derived from evidence from one merely compatible with it?"
19. **Fragmented-evidence retrieval under synthetic information overload:** Replay 04 V1 used a bounded decision-specific corpus. A harder controlled study could preserve the same critical decision evidence while embedding it in a much larger, mostly irrelevant environment, with facts fragmented across communications that separately establish an actor, amount, chronology, and continuing relevance. Prefer synthetic distractor messages over thousands of unrelated real personal emails for privacy and control. Illustrative scales could include 500, 2,000, 10,000, or 50,000 events; these are not frozen conditions. Measure visible critical-fact recovery, correct linking of fragments, chronology, downstream use, search or tool effort, unsupported synthesis, false-positive retrieval, confidence and calibration, and degradation as irrelevant information grows. Core question: "Can an agent reliably find and assemble consequential evidence when the information environment looks more like a real inbox than a curated decision packet?"
20. **Conflicting, stale, and superseded evidence:** Retrieval is insufficient when an agent finds facts but constructs the wrong current state of the world. Future controlled cases could include a fact later superseded, contradictory sources, a plan or negotiation posture mistaken for reality, historical values confused with current values, uncertain source authority, and chronology that determines which evidence should dominate. This complements rather than replaces item 1: Replay 01 focuses on operator claims, plans, posture, and claim-versus-fact discipline, while this family asks more broadly, "After retrieving evidence, can the agent correctly determine what is true now, what used to be true, what is merely claimed, and what remains uncertain?" Potential measurements include current-state reconstruction, temporal ordering, contradiction detection, source and provenance handling, explicit uncertainty, and downstream decision effects. No design is frozen here.
21. **Epistemic posture, contestability, and false authority:** This is distinct from Replay 01's interpretation of evidence about the world and operator; it examines how the agent presents its own knowledge and judgment. Controlled cases could include incomplete evidence, multiple reasonable interpretations, credible disagreement, questions partly determined by values, strong generic priors that conflict with user-specific evidence, user preferences that conflict with population defaults, situations where “I do not know” is appropriate, and system or institutional constraints on available recommendations. Measure whether established evidence, inference, uncertainty, prediction, model prior, preference or value judgment, and recommendation are distinguished; whether certainty is calibrated; whether contestability and alternatives are visible; whether unsupported conclusions gain false rhetorical authority; and whether users can identify where evidence ends and agent judgment begins. As a future governance motivation, model defaults, priors, policies, institutional choices, or value judgments could influence consequential decisions while appearing neutral; V1 did not test ideological bias, demonstrate institutional steering or deliberate manipulation, or empirically establish that risk. Core question: "When an agent's recommendation contains assumptions, priors, values, or judgment beyond the evidence itself, can the user see and contest them?"

## Conceptual Lenses

Possible framing, analytical vocabulary, and interface implications include the Plausibility Trap, AI workpapers, decision provenance, agent observability, retrieval as part of reasoning, the agent's reconstructed world model, personalization as more than memory, agent due diligence, historical replay as an agent-evaluation method, decision autopsy as a forensic metaphor, and evaluation of AI work rather than only AI answers. These are lenses for interpreting and designing later research, not separate experiments or a settled umbrella framework.
