Do not accept $220,000 immediately. After a same-day verification and market check, prepare a $235,000 counter while preserving the cash, no-inspection, no-contribution, two-week-close terms.

- $220,000 is roughly 12% below the intended $249,900 price after only about four active-market days.

- $235,000 is approximately the midpoint between offer and intended list price. It tests the buyer without countering near list or running a prolonged bidding process.
- A brief check of existing inquiries can reveal whether accepting $220,000 would unnecessarily leave money behind.
- A formal counter may legally terminate the original offer, so nothing should be countered until the signed contract, funds, earnest money, contingencies, title readiness, and seller net are reviewed.

If the buyer responds at $230,000 or more on the same verified terms, I would recommend accepting—subject to your separate express approval. If the buyer remains at $220,000, reassess it rather than rejecting it; it may still be attractive if no stronger interest exists.
